Strong 34% yoy revenue growth, driven by India and international segments

Higher GM and operating leverage drive 374 bps yoy EBITDA margin expansion

Lenskart reported overall gross margin of 70.3% in 1QFY27 (versus 68.7% in 1QFY26). The company managed to offset the currency headwind by increasing reliance on in-house manufacturing. Pre-Ind AS EBITDA margin was at 13.3%, registering a strong expansion of 374 bps yoy. Segmentally, (1) India business EBITDA margin expanded 197 bps yoy and pre-Ind AS EBITDA margin expanded 208 bps yoy and (2) international EBITDA margin increased 506 bps yoy and pre-Ind AS EBITDA margin expanded 620 bps yoy. EBITDA margin expansion was driven by gross margin expansion and operating leverage from marketing and other fixed expenses.

Healthy store expansion, large opportunity ahead

Other highlights include (1) India store count increasing to 2,725 from 2,609 stores in 4QFY26 and 2,137 stores in 1QFY26. Of its 2,725 stores, 1,067 stores were in metro cities and (2) international store count rising to 734 from 718 stores in 4QFY26 and 669 stores in 1QFY26. Stores were added across Japan, Southeast Asia and the Middle East. Lenskart is witnessing strong revenue ramp-up and SSSG in metro and non-metro stores in India. The company believes there is clear potential to add 10k+ stores in India across existing and new cities.

Key takeaways from earnings call

 Management guidance. Management maintained its guidance for long-term, steady-state pre-Ind AS

EBITDA margin of ~25%.

 Gross margin. A meaningful share of Lenskart’s frame procurement is sourced from China—so the

depreciation of INR against RMB exerts direct pressure on the cost of goods. Despite this, the product margin has continued to be resilient in both the India and international businesses through (1) growing premiumization and, increasingly, (2) a higher proportion of in-house manufacturing. At a consolidated level, the product margin crossed 70% for the first time this quarter, helped in part by a seasonally heavier international sunglasses mix.  Alongside its own manufacturing, vendor relationships and the volume-based cost efficiencies that come with scale are equally important levers to absorb input cost pressures. Management will focus on negotiations with vendors to improve product margins.

 Manufacturing. Lenskart’s long-term goal is self-reliance in frame and lens manufacturing. This

quarter, the company increased its investment in Baofeng Framekart, its China frame-manufacturing JV, to add in-house capacity. The Hyderabad facility will be the next major step-up in this journey.

 Eye tests. Lenskart conducts free eye tests to increase the size of the market. Management believes

eye testing is the biggest driver of the 23% volume growth in India. A significant majority of customers who do an eye test at Lenskart will purchase eyeglasses from the store, either immediately or within a few months. Management noted that customers who get an eye test for the first time take longer to make their purchase. It is possible that as the volume of eye tests increases, the conversion rates will drop and the company will have to work on improving rates.

 India business. In 1QFY27, the India business reported revenue growth of 31% yoy. The revenue

growth was primarily volume-driven, with eyewear units growing 23% yoy. This was powered by a 43% yoy increase in the eye tests conducted. ASP contributed a 6.4% increase yoy, driven by customers choosing premium lens options and not just by price increases. The SSSG came in at 18% in 1Q, driven by volume expansion from new customers acquired and also deepening relationships with gold members, driving repeat purchases. Gold membership now has 9 mn+ active members.  Market size. According to management, as India urbanizes, studies longer and lives on screens, the market size keeps growing. ~780 mn Indians need vision correction today, heading toward ~940 mn by FY2030E. Daily, 35k Indians get tested at Lenskart for the first time and discover they need prescription eyewear.  Growth. According to management, supply and not demand, is the constraint in India. Management noted that while there is a large opportunity for store expansion in Tier 2+ regions, it requires better understanding of the logistics, finding manpower for these stores, hiring optometrists and more. Remote optometry has enabled Lenskart to expand faster; management is also investing in AI eye testing to solve constraints related to Tier 2+ store expansion.  Network expansion (1/2). Lenskart has grown in three directions, i.e., (1) deeper in the pin codes it already served, (2) wider into unserved pin codes within existing cities and (3) into new towns. In the 1,517 pin codes that already had a Lenskart store in 2QFY26, density rose from 1.5 to 1.6 stores per pin code in nine months and the same stores kept growing, with SSSG of ~18% and SPSG of ~24%. Of the 2,821 unserved pin codes in 2QFY26 mapped in Lenskart’s existing cities, it entered 152 and opened 155 stores. 2,669 unserved pin codes remain, as of 1QFY27. It opened up stores in 140 Tier 2+ towns that Lenskart had never entered, with 142 stores in towns such as Hassan in Karnataka, Balasore in Odisha, Tura in Meghalaya and Gandhidham in Gujarat. According to GeoIQ, there are an additional 3,644 viable new Tier 2+ towns for Lenskart to enter.  Network expansion (2/2). Management noted that despite the net addition of 455 stores in the past nine months, it only covered ~5% of the 6,400 pin codes it had identified in 2QFY26. Management noted that most of its stores are still relatively young and can continue to grow at a healthy rate, given the high demand in India.

Owndays lenses have captured a significant share of the premium price point. Owndays premium lenses now generate over Rs15 bn annually in prescription eyeglass sales. Lenskart also has a luxury lens portfolio: Rodenstock and Tokai. Rodenstock is a century-old German optical house and Tokai is Japan's neuroscience-led lens maker. Volumes of this portfolio grew over 35% yoy combined in 1QFY27; together they generated Rs2.5 bn in prescription eyeglass sales last year. The frame portfolio is premiumizing too, with Meller on track to be a ~US$70 mn brand globally for sunglasses alone.  Progressive Lens. The progressive lens market has a distinct and growing share of volume, addressing presbyopia—where eyeglasses coverage in India is as low as ~25%. Lenskart has digitized progressive eye testing and units grew 33% yoy in 1QFY27.  Value-eyewear. The company has launched glasses priced at Rs500, sold profitably through Hustlr Club. According to management, lower-priced products are more margin accretive and will not dilute profitability as the business scales.  ASP. India ASP grew 6.4% yoy in 1Q to Rs1,856, driven by a continued shift in product mix toward premium products and progressive lenses.

 International business. Its revenue grew 38% yoy. While the business benefited from favorable

currency tailwinds, the underlying momentum remains strong. On a constant-currency basis, 1Q clocked revenue growth of ~29% yoy. This growth was broad-based, with robust performance across Japan, Southeast Asia, the Middle East and its other geographies. In the international business, the store addition will gradually accelerate once the unit economics improves.  Growth. Volume was the primary driver of growth; eyewear units grew 37.6% yoy. Additionally, sunglasses had a pronounced seasonal quarter, with units growing 58% yoy and nearly doubling sequentially, as April-June is the segment's seasonal peak, amplified by Meller's fast-growing online channel.  ASP. The ASP was roughly flat yoy at Rs5,267. On a constant-currency basis, ASP would have declined, reflecting this quarter's heavier sunglasses mix, a structurally lower-ASP category.  Profitability. According to management, international has structurally higher product margins than the India business, which should translate into even higher profitability as it scales. The international business has a higher product margin than the India business, as international ASP is 3X of India, while the ACP is 2X. In 1QFY27, the pre-Ind AS EBITDA margin expansion of 620 bps was driven by (1) operating leverage and (2) product margin expansion. The margin was also driven by higher sales of sunglasses this quarter, which carry a structurally higher margin.
